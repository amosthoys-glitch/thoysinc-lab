## The Room Bar Pro's USB-C, per the official specs

From Cisco's official data sheet (updated November 19, 2026):

- **One USB-C input:** accepts a laptop screen at up to **3840 × 2160 at 30 fps** (1920 × 1080 at 60 fps)
- **Requirement:** the laptop must support **video output over USB-C (DisplayPort Alt Mode)**
- **Two jobs on one cable:** the same USB-C cable handles **content sharing** and **USB passthrough**, which gives the laptop the Room Bar Pro's **camera, microphones and speakers**
- There is also **one HDMI input**, so in local (in-room) meetings you can show content from HDMI and USB-C together
- Shared content in a meeting goes up to **3840 × 2160 at 15 fps**
- For long runs, Cisco sells a **USB-C active optical cable (9 m / 29.5 ft)** as a separate part

## What to check when buying a long USB-C cable (general guidance)

- **Make sure it supports video.** USB-C cables that only charge or run at USB 2.0 speed will not carry a picture. Look for words like "Monitor," "Display" or "Video" and a listed resolution in the product name
- **Check the laptop too.** The laptop's USB-C port must support DisplayPort Alt Mode (or Thunderbolt)
- **Check the device specs first.** Meeting room gear has a maximum input resolution, so there is no need to pay more for numbers above it
- For short runs, see my [review of connecting an LG UltraWide monitor with an Anker USB-C cable](https://life.thoysinc.com/anker-usb-c-cable-lg-ultrawide/) (in Korean). That cable only comes in 3.3 ft, so **if you need a longer one**, you have to look at other brands, like this Silkland. To me, the Silkland feels like the same material as the Anker, and it is about as soft (or as stiff)
